LOFT × TUFTS CAPSTONE: Reimagining Recovery Tech
Project Overview
Context: Senior Capstone Project in Engineering Psychology | Tufts University × Loft Design (Spring 2026)

The Team: Ahmed Fouad, Andres Ruvalcaba, Fatima Tahir, William Bornmann

Our Core Focus: User research, concept generative planning, visual identity, and iterative UI/UX design.

The Challenge: High-precision fitness trackers overwhelm users with cold, clinical biometrics, inducing "data anxiety" when device metrics contradict how a user actually feels.

Advisors: Loft Design (Saloni Bedi, Clotilde Grimault, Sherry Eckholm) & Professor Nick Katis

Section 1: The Problem Space
Section Text
Modern health wearables are excellent at capturing data like HRV and sleep cycles, but they often fail at interpreting them empathetically. When a user wakes up feeling energized, but their device demands dozens of hours of rest due to a minor baseline dip, it creates an immediate cognitive disconnect.

Our target research confirmed that a staggering majority of young, active adults feel overwhelmed or judged by complex metrics. Instead of empowering users, current tracking models often lead to anxiety and metric avoidance.

Section 2: Research & Insights Synthesis
Section Text
To map the behavioral ecosystem of young athletes, we conducted preliminary field research at the gym followed by deep-dive user interviews to uncover true design friction.

During our transition from research to wireframing, Loft introduced us to the methodology of "Concept Cards." While we didn’t implement the cards as a final deliverable, they heavily inspired our concept planning phase. Taking inspiration from them forced us to move away from general data-dumping and instead distill abstract biometrics into distinct, feature-specific value propositions. This shifted our entire approach to iterative design, proving that individual features must be grounded in purposeful user needs before mapping a complex interface.

Section 3: The Interactive Ecosystem (The Solution)
Section Text
We designed an intuitive recovery system that repositions the wearable from a rigid clinical dashboard into an empathetic, supportive coach. By prioritizing a "recovery-first" philosophy, the experience bridges objective biometrics with subjective user intuition via a three-part user journey:

Body Literacy Check-In: Instead of forcing users to immediately digest raw data, the interface opens with a streamlined subjective check-in. Using responsive sliders, users quickly track real-time physical inputs like muscle soreness, fatigue levels, and mental state.

The Calibrated Balance: The system synthesizes hard sensor data with the user's reported feelings to build a balanced recovery output. If biometrics drop but your body feels fine, the UI actively validates that energy rather than triggering data panic.

Context-Adaptive Action Planning: To keep the features highly practical, we integrated a "Flexible Day vs. Fixed Schedule" toggle. For athletes with non-negotiable practices, switching to a fixed schedule shifts the system's smart suggestions from unhelpful "rest days" to active, actionable preparation, like targeted dynamic warm-ups or mobility work.

Section 4: Visual Strategy & Cognitive Offloading
Section Text
Our core design objective was reducing cognitive load. We worked through multiple design iterations to turn complex, clinical data tables into clean typography hierarchies, humanized microcopy, and intuitive visual metaphors.

By utilizing fluid layout elements and ambient visual states, the interface gives users an instant semantic understanding of their physiological readiness at a glance, eliminating the stress of parsing raw numbers.
